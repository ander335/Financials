# Financial Report Structure

Use this statement and metric guide when analyzing company reports.

The following statements normally appear together in the financial report. `Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income` does not contain significant information and can be skipped from analysis.

## Consolidated Balance Sheet

### Total Debt
- Includes short-term debt and current portion of long-term debt from the current or short-term liabilities section.
- Includes long-term debt and capital leases from long-term liabilities.

### Excess Cash
- Includes short-term investments only.
- Cash and cash equivalents should not be considered excess cash.

## Consolidated Income Statement

### Revenue
- Use total revenue from different business segments and services.

### Operating Income (EBIT)
- Use total revenue minus total operating costs.
- Do not include interest income, interest expense, or provision for income tax.

## Consolidated Statement of Cash Flow

### Depreciation and Amortization
- If depreciation and amortization are split into 2 different rows, sum them.

### Cash Flow From Operations
- Use the reported cash flow from operating activities.

### Capex
- Normally appears as `Purchases of property, plant and equipment` in cash flow from investing activities.
- Use a negative number.

### Debt Payment
- Include principal payment of debt, proceeds, repayments, issuance, and similar movements for both short-term and long-term debt.

### Dividends Paid
- Dividends paid to shareholders.
- Use a negative number.

## Other Information

### Shares Outstanding (Shares in Issue)
- Normally appears as `Diluted weighted-average number of common shares outstanding`.
- It may be found in `Consolidated Income statement`.
- It may be found in a separate `Earnings per share` section, or `Loss per share` if the company has no profit.
- It may also appear in a separate table that displays shares outstanding over years.
- Report the number in millions.
